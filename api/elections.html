Who Does the Work

Election Roles & Duties

Every role below is filled by citizens — most are paid, all are essential.

🗳️
Poll Worker
Paid

The frontline of democracy. Poll workers staff voting locations on Election Day, check in voters, distribute ballots, and ensure the polling place runs smoothly and lawfully.

  • Set up and close polling location
  • Verify voter registrations and issue ballots
  • Assist voters with accessibility needs
  • Operate voting equipment
  • Secure and transmit results at close
Pay$100–$200/day
Time12–14 hrs Election Day + training
WhoMust be registered voter in most states
⚖️
Election Judge / Inspector
Paid

Election judges oversee polling places and resolve disputes. Many states require bipartisan pairs — one from each major party — to serve together, ensuring accountability.

  • Supervise poll workers at the precinct
  • Rule on challenged ballots and voter eligibility
  • Enforce electioneering laws within the polling place
  • Certify precinct results at close of polls
Pay$150–$300/day
TimeElection Day + 2–4 hr training
WhoAppointed by county auditor or party
🏘️
Precinct Captain / Chair
Party Elected

The grassroots anchor of political organizing. Precinct captains know their neighborhood, mobilize voters, and serve as the link between residents and the party or campaign.

  • Know every registered voter in your precinct
  • Organize door-knocking and phone banking
  • Coordinate voter registration drives
  • Run the party caucus or precinct meeting
  • Report results to county party leadership
PayVolunteer
TimeYear-round, 2–10 hrs/week peak
WhoAny party member in the precinct
👁️
Party Observer / Watcher
Party Role

Observers represent a party or candidate inside the polling place and canvassing board. They watch the process without interfering — a critical transparency function.

  • Observe voter check-in and ballot issuance
  • Watch absentee ballot processing
  • Observe the canvass and vote count
  • Challenge irregularities through official channels
  • Report observations to party leadership
PayVolunteer (campaigns sometimes pay)
TimeElection Day, canvass board sessions
WhoCredentialed by party or candidate
📋
Voter Registration Volunteer
Volunteer

Drives registration at community events, schools, libraries, and door-to-door. The earlier voters are registered, the smoother Election Day runs. Nonpartisan and party-based opportunities exist.

  • Table at community events and markets
  • Assist new citizens and first-time voters
  • Verify and submit registration forms
  • Track and follow up on pending registrations
PayVolunteer
TimeFlexible, event-based
WhoAnyone 16+ in most states
📬
Absentee Ballot Processor
Sometimes Paid

As mail voting expands, counties need additional staff to open, verify, and process absentee ballots — often weeks before Election Day. Bipartisan teams required in most states.

  • Open and sort returned envelopes
  • Verify signatures against registration records
  • Cure deficient ballots by contacting voters
  • Feed ballots into tabulators on or after Election Day
Pay$15–$25/hr (county-dependent)
Time2–6 weeks before election
WhoHired by county auditor
🚗
GOTV Volunteer
Volunteer

Get Out The Vote volunteers contact identified supporters in the final days before the election, reminding them to vote and offering rides, childcare coordination, or absentee ballot help.

  • Phone bank and text bank from voter lists
  • Door-knock on Election Day (lit drop / knock & drag)
  • Drive voters to polls who lack transportation
  • Staff early voting and polling location drop-offs
PayVolunteer
TimeFinal 2 weeks + Election Day
WhoAnyone
🏛️
County Canvassing Board Member
Appointed / Elected

The canvassing board officially certifies election results at the county level. Members review provisional and challenged ballots, reconcile totals, and sign off on the official count.

  • Convene within days of election to canvass results
  • Adjudicate provisional and challenged ballots
  • Reconcile vote totals with ballots cast
  • Sign and certify the abstract of votes
PayStipend varies by county
TimeCanvass period (1–2 weeks post-election)
WhoBoard of supervisors or appointed
🔍
Nonpartisan Election Observer
Volunteer

Organizations like the League of Women Voters deploy nonpartisan observers to monitor elections for procedural compliance — not for any party, but for the integrity of the process itself.

  • Observe polling place procedures without interfering
  • Document wait times, accessibility issues, equipment problems
  • Report findings to observation organization
  • Testify or file reports if irregularities observed
PayVolunteer
TimeElection Day shifts
WhoCredentialed by civic organization
Iowa Featured

Iowa Election Infrastructure

Iowa's 99 counties each run their own elections under the supervision of the Iowa Secretary of State. The County Auditor is your primary contact for volunteering.

🌽 Iowa

Iowa administers elections through 99 County Auditors — one per county. They hire poll workers, manage voter rolls, process absentee ballots, and certify results to the State Canvassing Board.

🗓️Iowa Poll Worker Requirements

Must be a registered Iowa voter. Training provided by county. High school students 17+ may serve with county approval. Pay set by each county, typically $100–$150.

Iowa allows same-day registration at the polls. Online registration available through the SOS website. Volunteer registration drives coordinated through county parties and civic orgs.
