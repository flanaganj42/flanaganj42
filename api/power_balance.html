Historical Framework — Three Co-Equal Branches
The framers of the U.S. Constitution deliberately fragmented power to prevent tyranny. Three co-equal branches — Legislative, Executive, and Judicial — were granted distinct, enumerated powers. Congress alone writes law and controls the federal purse. The President executes law but cannot make it unilaterally. Courts interpret law but cannot enforce it. ◆ Each branch was given tools to limit the others. Congress can impeach the President and override vetoes. The President can veto legislation and nominate judges. Courts can strike down laws and executive actions as unconstitutional. ◆ James Madison warned in Federalist No. 47: "The accumulation of all powers, legislative, executive, and judiciary, in the same hands may justly be pronounced the very definition of tyranny." ◆ This design stood as the foundation of American democracy for over 230 years — a republic built on accountability, separation of powers, and the rule of law over any individual. ◆ Power flows from the people to their representatives — not downward from a single executive authority. The presidency was designed to be powerful but bound — answerable to Congress, the courts, and the Constitution itself. ◆ CIVIC RESILIENCE NETWORK · civicresilience.net · Civic Education Series ◆
