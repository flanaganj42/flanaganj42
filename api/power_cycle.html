Historical Framework — Three Co-Equal Branches
The framers of the U.S. Constitution deliberately fragmented power to prevent tyranny. Three co-equal branches — Legislative, Executive, and Judicial — were granted distinct, enumerated powers. Congress alone writes law and controls the federal purse. The President executes law but cannot make it unilaterally. Courts interpret law but cannot enforce it. ◆ Each branch was given tools to limit the others. Congress can impeach the President and override vetoes. The President can veto legislation and nominate judges. Courts can strike down laws and executive actions as unconstitutional. ◆ James Madison warned in Federalist No. 47: "The accumulation of all powers, legislative, executive, and judiciary, in the same hands may justly be pronounced the very definition of tyranny." ◆ This design stood as the foundation of American democracy for over 230 years — a republic built on accountability, separation of powers, and the rule of law over any individual. Power flows from the people to their representatives — not downward from a single executive authority. ◆ CIVIC RESILIENCE NETWORK · civicresilience.net · Civic Education Series ◆

Unitary Executive Theory — Checks & Balances Undermined
Recent Supreme Court decisions have fundamentally altered the balance of power the framers designed to prevent tyranny. In Trump v. United States (2024), the Court granted broad presidential immunity from criminal prosecution for official acts — a protection with no basis in the Constitution's text. ◆ In Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo (2024), the Court overturned the Chevron doctrine, stripping federal agencies of deference on regulatory interpretation and centralizing that authority in the judiciary. Congress's ability to delegate rule-making to expert agencies — built over a century — was dismantled overnight. ◆ The "major questions doctrine" further requires Congress to speak with extraordinary specificity before agencies can act — effectively requiring legislative supermajorities for any significant regulation, while handing courts a veto over democratic policy choices. ◆ The Unitary Executive Theory holds that all executive power is vested solely in the President, who may direct or remove any executive officer at will. The framers rejected this exact concentration of authority. They created independent offices, a divided legislature, and judicial independence precisely to prevent it. ◆ Madison wrote: "The accumulation of all powers in the same hands may justly be pronounced the very definition of tyranny." The question now before American citizens is whether the constitutional architecture will hold — and whether civic institutions can be strengthened to restore the balance the framers intended. ◆ CIVIC RESILIENCE NETWORK · civicresilience.net · Constitutional Crisis Series ◆
