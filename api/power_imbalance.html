Unitary Executive Theory — Checks & Balances Undermined
Recent Supreme Court decisions have fundamentally altered the balance of power the framers designed to prevent tyranny. In Trump v. United States (2024), the Court granted broad presidential immunity from criminal prosecution for official acts — a protection with no basis in the Constitution's text. ◆ In Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo (2024), the Court overturned the Chevron doctrine, stripping federal agencies of deference on regulatory interpretation and centralizing that authority in the judiciary. Congress's ability to delegate rule-making to expert agencies — built over a century — was dismantled overnight. ◆ The "major questions doctrine" further requires Congress to speak with extraordinary specificity before agencies can act — effectively requiring legislative supermajorities for any significant regulation, while handing courts a veto over democratic policy choices. ◆ The Unitary Executive Theory holds that all executive power is vested solely in the President, who may direct or remove any executive officer at will. The framers rejected this exact concentration of authority. They created independent offices, a divided legislature, and judicial independence precisely to prevent it. ◆ The practical result: a presidency increasingly insulated from legal accountability, a judiciary with expanded authority over national policy, and a Congress whose legislative power is hollowed out by executive orders and judicial override. This is not a partisan observation — it is a constitutional one. ◆ Madison wrote: "The accumulation of all powers in the same hands may justly be pronounced the very definition of tyranny." The question now before American citizens is whether the constitutional architecture will hold — and whether civic institutions can be strengthened to restore the balance the framers intended. ◆ CIVIC RESILIENCE NETWORK · civicresilience.net · Constitutional Crisis Series ◆
